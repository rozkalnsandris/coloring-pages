**Reasoning for 90 days:** a pragmatic *candidate* to preserve a Like across occasional return visits while limiting persistent identifiers. It is not a statutory safe harbor. Privacy by default, data minimization, alternative shorter durations, documented legitimate need and ease of withdrawal must be examined under GDPR Art. 5(1)(c),(e), 6, 7 and 13. A 90-day window must not silently roll forward on passive page loads or Print clicks. A renewed credential must require an independently supportable consent event.

## 2. Minimum sufficient technical implementation design (NOT CODE)

Use the existing `cloudflare/stats-worker.js` and D1 `visitor_credentials(visitor_hash,like_allowed,print_allowed,notice_version,consent_recorded_at,expires_at)` plus existing `likes` and `daily_visitors` tables; avoid a second database, manual IP logs, external analytics export, new perpetual pseudonymous tombstone table, or host-level cron by default.

**Issuance/renewal:** after a lawful explicit opt-in, a Worker-created 256-bit v3 token has `expires_at = unixepoch() + 7776000`. A server-side scope check continues to gate every Like/Print personalized **write**. Do not refresh expiry through mere visits, Like toggles or Print intents; determine a proportionate re-consent prompt. A newly minted credential on re-consent never revives expired/revoked token privileges. The proposed 90 days covers its *validity* and pseudonymous Like lifetime, not provider recovery histories.

**Scheduled cleanup candidate:** add a `scheduled()` handler to the *same* existing Worker, with **one UTC Cron Trigger** proposed at `10 0 * * *` (00:10 UTC). A Worker Cron Trigger is a Cloudflare runtime/configuration **mutation** and needs a separate exact owner gate. Its handler would run bounded, parameterized D1 statements, designed in one D1 `batch()` transaction:
1. Delete `likes` for credentials whose `expires_at <= unixepoch()`, so their existing delete trigger decrements `page_stats.like_count`.
2. Delete exactly the corresponding expired `visitor_credentials`. If step 1 fails, the batch must not leave only the registry deleted; test rollback and use no full-table/unbounded `DELETE`.
3. Delete HMAC `daily_visitors` day buckets older than yesterday UTC with the existing logical condition `day < date('now','-1 day')`. It is currently deleted only on successful `/visit`; a daily schedule removes reliance on future visits.

**Implementation qualification:** the above predicates are a contract sketch, **not reviewed/ready-to-run SQL**. Inspect exact D1/SQLite transactional semantics, volume, capacity, and table/trigger behavior; choose an explicitly bounded chunking strategy that never strands Likes after registry deletion. Old pre-v3/v2 Like rows lacking a credential registration **cannot** be caught by `expires_at` and need a separate data-subject-rights / legacy-retention decision, not a broad silent purge. D1's `batch()` is transactional according to Cloudflare documentation. No schedule, SQL, schema source file, Worker implementation or test changes are authorized by this design.

**Safety, verification and failure policy:** proposed source/integration tests must cover on-time expiration, boundary seconds, one- and two-purpose scope, like-counter triggers, Like revocation racing with scheduled cleanup, expired token replay, failed D1 statements/rollback, absent Cron invocation, missing DB binding, idempotent regular cleanup, HMAC boundary around UTC midnight, and legacy orphan identification. Observe aggregate counts and last successful cleanup **without adding visitor-identifying logs**; redacted read-only diagnostics only. A missed or failed Cron is not proof of deletion: flag a controller/operator alert, fail closed, and require separately authorized recovery before unexpected manual replay/alternate mutations. Establish actual schedule-delay tolerance, backlog policy and standing authority before enabling recurring writes.

**Subtlety — 48 hours vs two UTC days:** a row storing only `day=YYYY-MM-DD` lacks a true event timestamp. The proposed `day < date('now','-1 day')` predicate retains today's and yesterday's UTC buckets, so at 00:10 UTC the oldest stored HMAC might be approximately **48h10m old** at deletion, plus unbounded scheduler/incident delay. Avoid statements such as "all HMACs are deleted strictly within 48 hours". The honest policy is **two UTC calendar days with a documented scheduled-deletion lag**, or a separately reviewed timestamp-bearing schema and more frequent enforcement if strict 48h is legally required.

**D1 Time Travel/restoration boundary:** ordinary active-table deletion is not instant physical erasure from historical snapshots, edge logs or other processors. Restoration can reintroduce old rows/credentials unless a separately assessed restore-and-erasure-reconciliation procedure exists. Keep any production restore, backup wipe or credential controls behind their own owner/host gate; never claim irreversible erasure from a successful `DELETE`.

## 3. Audience assessment, not a legal shortcut

**Evidence from project source:** home landing copy offers "Malvorlagen für Kinder"; age badges include 3–6 and 4–8 years and the design is readily usable by children. A separate `kita.html` addresses kindergarten/preschool adults selecting and printing materials, and the A4/PNG print flow is plausibly adult-operated. The consent banner presently uses accessible informal German "du" and generic settings, **not a verified adult-only access gate**. Therefore a child-directed information-society service is a **realistic unresolved classification**, not resolved by page-title changes or a checkbox.

**Proposed product framing for owner/legal consideration:** free pictures remain accessible to everyone without identifying registration; explicitly present optional Like/Print tracking controls to adults acting as parents/guardians/educators, using clear German language (e.g. a review-only wording candidate: *"Die freiwilligen Datenschutz-Einstellungen sind für Eltern und pädagogische Fachkräfte gedacht. Malvorlagen kannst du ohne Einwilligung ansehen und ausdrucken."*). Do **not** infer adult status or consent from this sentence, do not silently auto-enable tracking, and do not collect children's names, birthdates, emails or parent identity documents solely to simplify the decision.

**Art. 8 legal decision tree:**
- **Not directly offered to children (evidence-based adult-directed service):** Art. 8's *specific parental authorization rule* may not apply, but GDPR Arts. 5–7/12–13, TDDDG §25, transparency, valid individual consent and risks affecting children still apply. Actual use/audience and UI must be consistent; written "adults only" is not sufficient.
- **Directly offered to children, processing their personal data on Art. 6(1)(a) consent:** for Germany, under-16 consent/authorization of the holder of parental responsibility and reasonable verification are required. An unverified "I am a parent" checkbox or a pseudonymous D1 timestamp is not itself adequate evidence; consider proportionate verification methods with expert guidance and minimal data collection.
- **Unresolved audience/consent:** do not release the optional persistent visitor-ID/Like/Print implementation. Keep publicly accessible anonymous browsing and A4/PNG printing, and avoid interpreting a refusal as consent for unrelated analytics.

**Owner/legal evidence needed:** intended users and marketing channels, observed/anticipated direct child usage, whether children themselves can use the Like/Print controls, exact consent text and version, age-appropriate transparency, consent proof/withdrawal strategy and adult/parental authorization mechanism (if Art. 8 applies). The content topic alone does not decide Art. 8; the **actual offer and basis for processing** do.
